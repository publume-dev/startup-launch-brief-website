- According to TechCrunch, Kyoto Fusioneering received grants from the U.S. Department of Energy and Tennessee to build the Unity-3 prototype fuel breeding blanket tester at Oak Ridge National Laboratory.
- The company is moving its U.S. headquarters to Oak Ridge, Tennessee.
- Unity-3 will test breeding blanket materials, including liquid lithium, which absorb heat and neutrons to generate tritium fuel and extract heat for power generation.
- A Fusion Industry Association survey found that more than half of fusion startups plan to work with external suppliers such as Kyoto Fusioneering on fuel cycle technologies.
- According to FusionX, Kyoto Fusioneering has raised $121 million in committed capital.
- No timeline for completing Unity-3 or initial test results has been disclosed, so the system's performance remains unproven.

Impact: Realta Fusion, Thea Energy, Type One Energy, and Xcimer Energy plan to use data from Unity-3 experiments to design their reactors.
